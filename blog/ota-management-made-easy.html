OTA management, listing and optimisation made easy

Online travel agencies (OTAs) such as Booking.com, Expedia and Agoda bring hotels guests from all over the world. They also bring commission, rules, and the risk of selling the same room twice. Done well, OTA management is mostly a routine. Here is how to set it up.

Why OTAs still matter

International travellers, and many local ones, start their search on an OTA. Even guests who end up booking directly often find you there first. The aim is not to avoid OTAs, but to use them well and not depend on them for everything.

Getting the listing right

Photos

Photos are the single biggest factor in whether a guest clicks. Use bright, current, professional photos of every room type, the bathroom, the view, the restaurant, the pool and the entrance. Put the best one first. Show the room the guest will actually get.

Room names and mapping

Use clear names ("Deluxe King Room with Balcony") rather than internal codes. Each OTA room must be mapped to the right room type in your system; a wrong mapping is the most common cause of overbookings.

Description and facilities

Tick every facility you have: Wi-Fi, parking, airport transfer, generator, breakfast, pool, gym, meeting rooms. Guests filter by these, and a missing tick hides you from their search. Write a short description that says who the hotel is for and what is nearby.

Policies

State check-in and check-out times, children and extra-bed rules, deposit and cancellation terms clearly. Surprises lead to bad reviews.

What helps you rank

Each OTA has its own ranking, but they reward broadly the same things:

  • a complete listing with good photos;
  • competitive and consistent prices across channels;
  • open availability, especially for the dates guests search most;
  • a strong review score with recent reviews;
  • a low cancellation rate and few complaints;
  • fast replies to guest messages.

Visibility programmes and higher commission can push you up the list, but they work best once the basics are in place.

Rates and policies

Offer at least two rates: a flexible rate and a slightly cheaper non-refundable one. Add a minimum stay over peak dates and a long-stay discount if it suits you. Change prices by season and demand, and keep them in step across channels so no OTA undercuts your own website.

Use a channel manager

If you are on more than one OTA, updating each one by hand is how overbookings happen. A channel manager connects your system to all of them:

  • when a room sells anywhere, it closes everywhere;
  • prices, minimum stays and stop-sells are set once and pushed to every channel;
  • OTA bookings, changes and cancellations arrive in your reservations automatically.

Azeli PMS connects to the OTAs through a certified channel manager. OTA bookings appear in Reservations within about a minute, a room that is blocked for repairs comes off sale on every channel at once, and the commission each OTA charges is accrued in your accounts every night, so you always know what the channel really costs.

Reviews

Reply to every review within a few days. Thank the guest, address the problem if there was one, and say what you have done about it. Future guests read your replies as much as the reviews. Ask happy guests for a review the day after check-out; a short WhatsApp with a direct link works well.

Keep commission in check

OTA commission commonly runs from around 15% to 20% or more of the booking. You will not avoid it, but you can reduce how much of your business pays it:

  • make your own website book rooms, at a price no higher than the OTAs;
  • give guests a reason to book direct next time, such as breakfast or a late check-out;
  • build corporate and repeat-guest business that books with you;
  • ask guests who booked through an OTA to save your WhatsApp number for next time.

A weekly routine

WhenTask
DailyCheck new OTA bookings and messages; reply to guests.
Twice a weekCheck prices for the next 60 days against demand and competitors.
WeeklyReply to new reviews; check for mapping errors or closed dates.
MonthlyReview channel mix and commission; refresh photos and content if needed.
